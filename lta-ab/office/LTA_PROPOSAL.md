## Timing and process

- The consultation ran from 8 Oct 2026, 1700hrs, to 2 Nov 2026, 2359hrs (NR para 1, 8).
- It is part of LTA's review of the COE framework (NR para 1).
- LTA had engaged more than 200 members of the public, academics and industry
  representatives, including focus groups in April and May 2026 (NR para 4).
- The review was expected to finish by end-2026, with findings and recommendations by
  1H2027 (NR para 9).

## The objective as LTA put it

- The aim was to "more effectively distinguish between mass-market and higher-value cars in
  the COE system, while allowing COE prices to continue to be determined by market forces"
  (NR para 2).

## Reasons LTA gave

1. Engine capacity and power had been the A/B criteria "since 2014" (NR para 3).
2. "Advances in vehicle technology, particularly with the advent of electric vehicles" had
   made these criteria "less effective". Manufacturers were "increasingly able to adjust
   vehicle specifications to fit within existing Category A thresholds" (NR para 3).
3. As a result, "more car buyers are competing for Category A COEs, narrowing the price gap
   between Categories A and B" (NR para 3).
4. **The one statistic LTA cited:** "Between February 2026 to June 2026, Category A COE prices
   have exceeded Category B prices on three occasions" (NR para 3). Annex B puts it as
   "three occasions in 2026" (B, top panel). The public bidding data can check this.
5. Annex B summarised the current distinction as "becoming less meaningful", with criteria
   "increasingly ineffective" (B, top panel).
6. The merger-plus-mechanism idea came from engagement participants. It was "similar to a
   suggestion put forth by" Mr Edward Chia, Deputy Chairperson of the GPC (Transport)
   (NR para 4).

## The proposed design

- **Merge (B, panel 1):** "All passenger cars, whether mass-market or higher-value, bid from
  the same COE pool"; "One prevailing COE price, determined by demand and available COE
  supply".
- **Feebate (B, panel 2):** "A rebate or surcharge, based on the value of the car, is
  applied to the prevailing COE price". Lower-value cars get a rebate, mid-range cars no
  adjustment, higher-value cars a surcharge.
- **Band assignment (C, steps 1-2):**
  - Take the OMVs from past registrations of the same car model and find the median.
  - Compare that model's median OMV against "OMV percentile thresholds" to set its band.
  - The illustration was five bands: 1-2 rebate, 3 no adjustment, 4-5 surcharge. Its
    figures ($31,000 to $38,000, median $34,000) were marked "for illustrative purposes
    only".
- **Cut-offs and amounts (C, step 3):**
  - Bands were "determined based on the percentile of vehicles registered in the previous
    year".
  - Fee and rebate amounts "take reference from historical price gaps between Categories A
    and B".
  - Both were to be "reviewed annually".
- **Category E:** LTA asked for views on adjusting it if A and B merge (NR para 5; A, item 2).
  No design was given in the release or annexes.

## What LTA asked (Annex A, item 1, plus item 2)

| Q | Question (Annex A wording, abridged) |
|---|---|
| Q1 | "Whether a single passenger car category with a feebate system should be introduced to maintain differentiation between mass-market and higher-value cars" |
| Q2 | Whether median OMV is "an appropriate basis for determining the applicable feebate band" |
| Q3 | Whether "a three-band or five-band structure is preferred" |
| Q4 | Whether "the feebate should apply to COE renewals" |
| Q5 | "How Category E should be adjusted if Categories A and B are merged" (item 2) |

## Data LTA cited

- The only statistic was the count of three exercises, February to June 2026, in which
  Category A's premium exceeded Category B's (NR para 3).
- No series, chart or OMV distribution appeared in the release or annexes.
- Annex C's OMV figures were illustrative.

## Other suggestions LTA reported and answered (Annex A, "Other Suggestions We Heard")

1. **Allocation by family needs.** LTA said this would need "difficult judgements" on
   priority, and household circumstances change over a COE's life.
2. **Individuals bidding directly.** LTA said it was "looking at making it easier".
3. **Pay-as-you-bid.** LTA said it was "unlikely to materially change bidding behaviour,
   allocation outcomes, or overall COE price levels", because "most bids today already
   cluster around the eventual clearing price".
4. **A private-hire car category.** LTA said it would take quota from A and B and need rules
   on use.
5. **A multiple-car surcharge.** LTA said multiple-car owners "are not a key driver of
   demand", and such a surcharge would be hard to administer.

These are outside Q1-Q5 and outside the note.
