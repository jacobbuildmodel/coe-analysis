## Summary

LTA proposes one car COE category, with a rebate or surcharge set by model value.

- **Named cars:** the two BYD Sealion 7 versions would be $15,000 apart under three bands and $7,500 under five (as proposed, not final).
- **Record:** Category A cleared above Category B in 21 of 397 exercises, January 2010 to October 2026, exercise 1.
- **Balance:** at the 2025 mix behind LTA's tables, surcharges exceed rebates by $2,250 a car on average; a starting point, not a forecast.

## What is LTA deciding?

A feebate on the COE price (paragraph 2.9), banded by each model's median Open Market Value (OMV) as a percentile of 2025 registrations (paras 2.15-2.18):

- **Option A:** 0-35th percentile, $15,000 rebate; 35th-50th, $0; above 50th, $15,000 surcharge.
- **Option B:** 0-10th, $15,000 rebate; 10th-35th, $7,500 rebate; 35th-50th, $0; 50th-75th, $7,500 surcharge; above 75th, $15,000 surcharge.

Views close 2 November 2026, 23:59; findings are expected in the first half of 2027.

## What would it mean for named cars?

The BYD Sealion 7 Dynamic sits in Band 2 (Option A) or Band 3 (Option B), the Performance in Band 3 or Band 4: $15,000 apart under three bands, $7,500 under five.

## What the bidding record shows

Category A cleared above B in 21 of 397 exercises from January 2010 to October 2026, exercise 1, the last in this record; 3 were in 2026 (lta-ab/out/a_above_b.csv; lta-ab/NOTE.pdf).

## Where the design could go wrong

**(a) Balance.** Of the 2025 registrations behind LTA's tables, about 50 per cent sit in surcharge bands and 35 per cent in rebate bands, so at that mix surcharges exceed rebates by $2,250 a car on average under both options. This is the starting point, not a forecast: once set, a model's band follows its value, and the feebate is meant to move buyers towards rebate cars, shrinking the net, possibly reversing it. Bands are set by OMV percentile and reviewed annually (para 2.15), method not stated; if cut-offs are re-set by percentile at each review, the tilt returns each time. A centred design starts neutral; only a yearly published balance shows where it stands.

A merged premium clears where one quota meets all bids, so it need not equal the A and B average. A Straits Times illustration states "the proposed combined COE price is the average of Cat A and B premiums" (ST Instagram, 18 October 2026); the illustration assumes this. This page gives no direction for it; a sealed prediction follows in December.

**(b) Category E.** Category E certificates register any car (para 2.21). Since May 2022 Category E cleared at or above both A and B in 74 of 107 exercises, a median $888 above B: far below a $15,000 surcharge. The paper does not say whether the feebate applies to such cars. Unless the feebate follows the car, Category E could become the cheaper route for the dearest cars.

**(c) Cliffs.** At each cut-off, a model just above pays a full step more: $15,000 under three bands, $7,500 under five. Elsewhere, car tax thresholds drew models to their cheaper side (Sallee and Slemrod 2012; Ito and Sallee 2018).
